Expert Calls & Meetings: The Fastest Way to Get Real Industry Answers

Learn how direct conversations with experienced industry professionals can help businesses replace assumptions with current, practical insight for research and strategy.

Research & Insights · Expert Calls & Meetings

What Do We Mean by “Expert Calls & Meetings”?

An expert call is a scheduled one-on-one (or small group) conversation between a business decision-maker and an industry professional who has direct, current experience in a specific field. It could be a former hospital procurement manager explaining how medical devices actually get approved for purchase, a supply chain veteran walking through the real bottlenecks in a category, or a regional operator describing what is happening on the ground in a market that looks completely different on paper.

Unlike a survey or a published report, an expert meeting is a live, two-way conversation. You can ask follow-up questions. You can push back on an answer. You can steer the discussion toward the exact detail that matters for your decision. That flexibility is the entire value proposition.

Key idea: An expert meeting gives you the opportunity to ask targeted questions and explore details that published information may not capture.

Why Expert Calls & Meetings Matter More Than Ever

A few forces have made expert conversations more important, not less, even in an age of abundant data.

Current context can add depth to published research

Information moves faster than reports get published. By the time a market research report is printed, the market has often already shifted. An expert who is working in the field right now can tell you what changed recently, not just what was documented previously.

Public data can be noisy. Published numbers and commentary vary in methodology and quality. A knowledgeable expert can add context: why a number looks the way it does, what assumptions sit behind it, and what factors could change it.

Nuance does not fit in a spreadsheet. Regulatory quirks, buyer psychology, seasonal patterns, and internal industry dynamics may not appear clearly in public data, but they can emerge in a real conversation with someone who lives inside that world.

Speed can matter. When teams need a focused answer, an expert conversation can be one way to compress part of the research process.

The Usual Challenges of Setting Up Expert Meetings

If expert calls are so useful, why doesn't every team run them constantly? In practice, a few obstacles get in the way:

  • Finding the right person. Not every “expert” is actually close enough to the day-to-day reality you need. Finding someone with the exact, narrow experience you're after can take days of cold outreach.
  • Vetting and compliance. Especially in regulated industries like healthcare, finance, or pharma, you need to confirm the expert isn't bound by conflicts of interest or confidentiality restrictions before the call happens.
  • Scheduling friction. Coordinating time zones, calendars, and NDAs can turn a simple 30-minute call into a two-week ordeal.
  • Inconsistent quality. Without a structured process, some calls turn out to be a waste of time because the “expert” wasn't quite the right fit after all.

What to Prepare Before an Expert Call

A great expert meeting is rarely accidental. A little preparation goes a long way:

  • Write down your top 3 to 5 questions in advance. Open-ended chats can be pleasant but inefficient. A tight list keeps the call focused.
  • Share context with the expert ahead of time (where appropriate). A short brief on what you're trying to figure out helps the expert prepare useful, specific answers instead of generic ones.
  • Decide who needs to be in the room. A single decision-maker asking sharp questions can be more focused than a large group of passive listeners.
  • Leave room for follow-up. Some of the best insight comes from a question you didn't plan to ask, prompted by something the expert just said.

Getting the Most Out of Every Expert Meeting

Turn the conversation into a usable research input

A few habits can help teams get more value from expert calls:

  • Record the call with permission so nothing important gets lost in note-taking.
  • Ask the expert what they would ask if they were in your position. Experts can surface risks you haven't thought to look for.
  • Compare notes across multiple calls rather than relying on a single conversation, especially for high-stakes decisions.
  • Follow up with a short thank-you and, if useful, a specific question that came up after the call ended.

Final Thoughts

Expert calls and meetings work because they replace assumptions with direct, current, human experience. In fast-moving markets, that kind of firsthand insight can complement published research and other sources of evidence.
